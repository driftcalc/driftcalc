Has the three-fund portfolio beaten just buying America?

July 1990 to September 2025, 35 years of monthly total returns. Both mixes rebalanced once a year, both measured over the same months.

The answer
2.9 pts a year
Over 35 years, US total market returned 10.99% a year against 8.13% for the three-fund portfolio. That is 2.9 percentage points a year, and on $10,000 left alone it is the difference between $394,283 and $157,171. It also fell further on the way: 50.3% against 27.1%.
Nominal returns unless the row says otherwise. No fund fees, spreads or taxes.

What this leaves out

The window is doing a lot of the work. These are the months where every series involved exists, which is July 1990 onward. Pick a different start and the answer can change sides. That is a fact about backtests, not about these two mixes.

Nobody earned these numbers. They are index returns. A real holder paid a fund fee, paid a spread on every trade, paid tax on dividends in a taxable account, and rebalanced late or not at all.

The worst fall is the part that decides it. A number you can read in a second took years to sit through, and the mix someone can hold through a fall beats the one they sell.
