What did 60/40 cost against all stocks?

May 1953 to September 2025, 72 years of monthly total returns. Both mixes rebalanced once a year, both measured over the same months.

The answer
2.0 pts a year
Over 72 years, US total market returned 11.35% a year against 9.31% for a 60/40 portfolio. That is 2.0 percentage points a year, and on $10,000 left alone it is the difference between $24,084,319 and $6,309,407. It also fell further on the way: 50.3% against 30.0%.
Nominal returns unless the row says otherwise. No fund fees, spreads or taxes.

What this leaves out

The window is doing a lot of the work. These are the months where every series involved exists, which is May 1953 onward. Pick a different start and the answer can change sides. That is a fact about backtests, not about these two mixes.

Nobody earned these numbers. They are index returns. A real holder paid a fund fee, paid a spread on every trade, paid tax on dividends in a taxable account, and rebalanced late or not at all.

The worst fall is the part that decides it. A number you can read in a second took years to sit through, and the mix someone can hold through a fall beats the one they sell.
