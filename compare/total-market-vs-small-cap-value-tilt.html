Did a small-cap value tilt pay?

July 1926 to September 2025, 99 years of monthly total returns. Both mixes rebalanced once a year, both measured over the same months.

The answer
1.1 pts a year
Over 99 years, a small-value tilt returned 11.38% a year against 10.32% for US total market. That is 1.1 percentage points a year, and on $10,000 left alone it is the difference between $441,493,641 and $170,640,920. It also fell further on the way: 84.4% against 83.7%.
Nominal returns unless the row says otherwise. No fund fees, spreads or taxes.

What this leaves out

The window is doing a lot of the work. These are the months where every series involved exists, which is July 1926 onward. Pick a different start and the answer can change sides. That is a fact about backtests, not about these two mixes.

Nobody earned these numbers. They are index returns. A real holder paid a fund fee, paid a spread on every trade, paid tax on dividends in a taxable account, and rebalanced late or not at all.

The worst fall is the part that decides it. A number you can read in a second took years to sit through, and the mix someone can hold through a fall beats the one they sell.
