The thing the whole strategy rests on

HFEA needs the bond leg to rise when the stock leg falls. That is a correlation, and a correlation is a fact about a period, not a property of the assets.

The honest version

The window everyone posts is the one where it worked. Stocks and long Treasuries moved opposite ways in about a third of the rolling three-year windows since 1953. The 2010s are the most negative decade in the data. Build a case on that decade and you have built it on the exception.

This model cannot see the thing that would actually kill you. UPRO and TMF reset daily, so what ends a 3x fund is one day down more than a third, not one month. Everything here is monthly, which means it quietly assumes that day never arrives.

The legs are stand-ins. UPRO tracks the S&P 500 and this uses the US total market. TMF tracks the ICE 20+ Year index and this uses a modelled rolling par 20-year Treasury, which for the years Treasury issued no 20-year is really the 30-year. Fund fees here are the 0.90% the rest of the site uses; UPRO and TMF both charge more.

Quarterly rebalancing is the easy part to write down. Every number above assumes you sold the leg that went up and bought the leg that was collapsing, on schedule, for decades, including in the quarters where both legs fell. That is the part people do not do.

None of this says the strategy is wrong. It says the evidence for it is one favourable stretch, and the page you are reading is the rest of the record.
